# Coach sheet: read this in 3 minutes

The clock times come from PITCH.md. If the new slides move them, keep the moment and ignore the number.
Each tip is marked **[research]**, with a source, or **[practice]**, which is coaching habit with no study behind it.

## 10 tips for tonight
1. **Title, before 0:00: stand still, look at the judges for 2 s, then give the hook.** Say the product's name: nobody says "Ctrl+Voice" aloud anywhere in the current script. [research] Judges form impressions in seconds: silent clips under 30 s, and even 6 to 15 s, predicted teachers' end-of-term ratings (Ambady & Rosenthal 1993, JPSP).
2. **Chat, 0:03 to 0:53: keep quiet while the audio plays.** "Which two?" is your first peak, so ask it, wait, and don't explain. [research] Narration that duplicates what the audience is already taking in lowers learning (Mayer, Heiser & Lonn 2001, redundancy). Applying that to a pitch is my inference.
3. **Map, 1:03 to 2:10: this is the biggest risk in the talk.** Nine regions in 67 s, each with jargon. Cut the second technical detail from each line:
   - "CTC-ით" and "ენის მოდელის გარეშე" (Align);
   - "35 მილიარდი პარამეტრი…" (Decide);
   - "MASK ტოკენებს…" (Re-say).
   
   Always use the short versions for regions 6 and 8, not only when you are late. Keep the *why* lines, Guard and Verify: they are what makes judges trust the system. The numbers go to Q&A. [research] Same multimedia-load principle as tip 2 (Mayer). It also buys back the 3 s the chat already runs over, plus the time for the new slides.
4. **Map, region 8: drop "AUC 0.88".** It invites "how good is it in free speech?", and the honest answer is 19% (Q&A sheet 6). The feature is also switched off in the live demo. [practice]
5. **2:10: the time gates are not optional.** People remember a talk's strongest moment and its ending much more than its length or its middle. A demo beat you skip costs little; a close you rush costs a lot. [research] The peak-end rule (Fredrickson & Kahneman 1993; a 2022 meta-analysis of 174 effects supports it in the lab, but field evidence is mixed).
6. **3:26, after the replaced word plays: "ეს სიტყვა მე არასდროს მითქვამს."** Then hold 2 s of silence before Tone. This is the peak of the talk, so let it land. [research] Peak-end, as in tip 5.
7. **4:25, Bingo: stamp only what fired.** If one didn't, say „დღეს ოთხიდან სამი“ calmly. [research] In a lab experiment and a field study, investors' funding decisions followed perceived *preparedness*, not displayed passion (Chen, Yao & Kotha 2009, Academy of Management Journal). Being visibly honest and prepared beats hype.
8. **4:40, close: watch the phrase "about a second".** The room has just watched a ~10 s wait. Stress the scope, "the ums and the take-backs", and you can add the fact from Q&A 2: "a re-said word takes about 3 s". [practice]
9. **Last 10 s: end on the tagline and the name, not on the GitHub line.** Then stop talking. [research] Peak-end, as in tip 5. The new wording is below.
10. **Q&A: give a one-sentence answer, then one piece of evidence, then stop.** "We haven't measured that" is a complete answer. [practice] I could not verify a study for this tonight, so treat it as coaching habit.

## First 10 seconds
> *(stand still, look at the judges, 2 s)* „ხმოვანი მესიჯი ჩაგიწერიათ… მოგისმენიათ… და მერე წაგიშლიათ?“ *(pause, raise your own hand)* „ყველას გვიქნია.“ *(beat)* „ეს არის Ctrl+Voice.“ **→**

[Ever recorded a voice message… listened back… and deleted it? *(pause)* We all have. *(beat)* This is Ctrl+Voice.]

## Last 10 seconds (after the three numbers)
> „ყველაფერი GitHub-ზეა: დაასკანერეთ QR კოდი და სცადეთ.“ *(beat)* „Ctrl+Voice. თქვი არეულად… გაგზავნე სუფთად.“ *(1 s)* „მადლობა.“ *(stop, smile, wait)*

[It's all on GitHub: scan the QR code and try it. *(beat)* Ctrl+Voice. Say it messy… send it clean. *(1 s)* Thank you.]

The order changes so the tagline comes last. "QR კოდი" is clearer than "კოდი", which can also mean source code.

## If the demo fails
1. Say what happened in **one** calm line, with **one** joke (use the existing „სერვერმა ძილისთვის კარგი მომენტი შეარჩია.“). Don't debug on stage and don't apologise twice. [research] After someone has shown competence, a small blunder can make them *more* likeable (Aronson et al. 1966, the pratfall effect: one old study, with mixed follow-ups). By 2:10 you have already shown competence.
2. Keep to the gates: at 25 s, **Alt+Tab, then B**, and say „ადრინდელი გაშვებიდან“, never „ლაივი“.
3. Go back to the story: stamp only what really happened, then give the close **on time**. The ending is what they remember, not the glitch.

## The 5 most likely judge questions (answer first, then one fact)
**1. Georgian?**
„დღეს მხოლოდ ინგლისური, და ყველაზე მეტად ქართული გვინდა. Whisper და Qwen მრავალენოვანია; ახალ ენას სჭირდება ახალი ალაინერი, ახალი წარმოთქმის მოდელი და ახალი წესები „um“-ისა და „scratch that“-ისთვის.“

[English only today, and Georgian is the one we want most. Whisper and Qwen are multilingual; a new language needs a new aligner, a new pronunciation model and new rules for "um" and "scratch that".]

**2. Isn't this a deepfake tool?**
„არა: მხოლოდ შენი ხმა, მხოლოდ ის სიტყვები, რაც გინდოდა, და ყველა AI სიტყვა მწვანით ჩანს. ორიგინალი ფაილი არასდროს იცვლება. გეგმაშია AudioSeal-ის ვოტერმარკი ყველა გენერირებულ სიტყვაზე და მსმენელისთვის ხილული შენიშვნა „შეცვლილი სიტყვები“.“

[No: only your voice, only the words you meant, and every AI word shown in green. The original file is never changed. Planned: an AudioSeal watermark on every generated word and a "words changed" note the listener can see.]

**3. What did *you* build? The models already exist.**
„ცალკეული მოდელები ცნობილია: Whisper, Qwen, PlayDiffusion, F5-TTS. ჩვენია ის, რაც მათ შორისაა: Whisper-ს „um“-ებით ვაპრომპტავთ, LLM-ს მხოლოდ წაშლა შეუძლია და წესები ამოწმებს, ორ ხმის მოდელს ორჯერ ვამოწმებთ, და ვსვამთ მხოლოდ ახალ სიტყვას. ასე ხმის მსგავსება 0.98-ია; მთლიანი გენერაციისას 0.73-მდე ეცემა.“

[The individual models are known ones. Ours is what sits between them: we prompt Whisper with ums, the LLM can only delete and the rules check it, both voice models are checked twice, and we splice in only the new word. That keeps voice similarity at 0.98; regenerating the whole clip drops it to 0.73.]

**4. What if it cuts something important?**
„მხოლოდ წაშლა შეუძლია, და სახიფათო სიტყვები დაცულია: „არა“, რიცხვი, თარიღი ან დრო მხოლოდ მაშინ ქრება, თუ იმავე ტიპის შესწორება მოსდევს. საეჭვო რჩევად რჩება. ათ მახე წინადადებაზე აზრი არცერთში შეცვლილა.“

[It can only delete, and the risky words are protected: a "not", a number, a date or a time only goes if a same-type repair replaces it. Anything uncertain becomes a suggestion. On 10 trick sentences, none changed meaning.]

**5. What does it cost, and what's the business?**
„მესიჯის ფასი ჯერ არ დაგვითვლია. ყველაფერი, LLM-ის ჩათვლით, ერთ GPU-ზე მუშაობს: ტოკენზე მხოლოდ 3 მილიარდი პარამეტრია აქტიური, მძიმე ნაწილები კი მხოლოდ საჭიროებისას ირთვება. შემდეგი ნაბიჯი მოწყობილობაზე გადატანაა, და მაშინ სერვერის ხარჯი ქრება.“

[We haven't priced it per message yet. One GPU runs everything, the LLM included: only 3B parameters are active per token, and the heavy steps run only when used. The next step is on-device, and then the server cost goes away.]

## Body language and voice
These are all **[practice]**.
- Keep your feet planted while you talk. Move only at transitions, such as walking to the laptop.
- Talk to the judges, not to the projector. Turn to the screen only to point, then turn back.
- End your own Georgian sentences with a **falling** pitch. Don't uptalk: your product's Confident feature makes exactly this point.
- Pause for a full beat after „ხმის — ვერა.“, „აქამდე.“, „ეს სიტყვა მე არასდროს მითქვამს.“ and „ბინგო.“.
- Speak louder and slower than feels natural, especially on the map: one idea per breath.

## Sources and how strong they are
- Fredrickson & Kahneman 1993, JPSP, duration neglect and peak-end. A 2022 meta-analysis (174 effect sizes) supports it in the lab; field evidence is mixed. [https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/fredr-kahneman-jpsp1993.pdf](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/fredr-kahneman-jpsp1993.pdf), [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak%E2%80%93end_rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak%E2%80%93end_rule)
- Ambady & Rosenthal 1993, JPSP 64(3), thin slices. It predicts *ratings*, not real teaching quality. [https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~bishop/classes/math598.F25/Half_a_Minute.pdf](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~bishop/classes/math598.F25/Half_a_Minute.pdf)
- Chen, Yao & Kotha 2009, Academy of Management Journal: preparedness, not passion, predicted funding decisions. Later work (Cardon et al. 2016) disputes how passion was measured. [https://effectuation.org/hubfs/Journal%20Articles/2017/05/Cardon_et_al-2016-Entrepreneurship_Theory_and_Practice-1.pdf](https://effectuation.org/hubfs/Journal%20Articles/2017/05/Cardon_et_al-2016-Entrepreneurship_Theory_and_Practice-1.pdf)
- Mayer, Heiser & Lonn 2001, the redundancy effect. This is learning research, and applying it to pitches is my inference. [https://www.csus.edu/indiv/e/eppersonm/hist107/documents/mayer,%20heiser,%20&%20lonn%20(2001).pdf](https://www.csus.edu/indiv/e/eppersonm/hist107/documents/mayer,%20heiser,%20&%20lonn%20(2001).pdf)
- Aronson, Willerman & Floyd 1966, the pratfall effect. One small study, and later results depend on context. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratfall_effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratfall_effect)
- Bruine de Bruin 2005, Acta Psychologica: later slots scored higher in Eurovision and figure skating. You can't control your slot; it is one more reason to make the ending strong. [https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/save-the-last-dance-for-me-unwanted-serial-position-effects-in-ju/](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/save-the-last-dance-for-me-unwanted-serial-position-effects-in-ju/)
- Hackathon rubrics differ by event. The common pattern is novelty, technical implementation and impact, often weighted about equally (e.g. [https://devforum.okta.com/t/what-are-the-judging-criteria-for-the-hackathon/15842](https://devforum.okta.com/t/what-are-the-judging-criteria-for-the-hackathon/15842)). In this talk, impact is the stat slide, technical depth is the map and Q&A, and execution is the live demo.
- Folk advice with no source I could verify tonight: "answer first", "stories beat statistics" (the often-quoted 63%/5% figure from *Made to Stick* is a classroom exercise, not a study), and all of the body-language tips.
