Architecture
The whole pipeline

Ctrl+Voice

The diagram draws itself.

“Here’s the whole system. The app on your laptop streams the microphone over a WebSocket. Every 0.7 seconds Whisper re-transcribes, and a second model re-times every word.

An LLM decides what to change, but it never touches audio: it only picks words, and hard-coded validators check every choice. Then there are three paths: cut, re-say a word in your voice, or restyle. Everything lands in an edit list. Your original recording is never modified, and the renderer builds the message only when you play or send it.”

Architecture
Step 1: hear every word

Ctrl+Voice

Every 0.7 s, Whisper large-v3-turbo re-transcribes everything said so far. A filler-heavy prompt makes it write the ums it hears.

Its word times drift, so a wav2vec2 character CTC model re-times every word: Viterbi forced alignment on its CTC trellis.

That model has no language model, so it spells what you really said. An extra filler-shaped word that the alignment can’t explain is an um Whisper dropped, and we put it back.

Whisper“Nikoloz, big news”
CTCNICLOS ON BIG NEWS
Ours“Nikoloz, um, big news”

“Step one: hear every word, exactly where it is. While you talk, we stream 24 kHz audio in 100 ms chunks, and every 0.7 seconds Whisper large-v3-turbo re-transcribes everything so far. We prompt it with filler-heavy text, so it writes the ums it hears.”

Click: “But look at the grey boxes: Whisper’s word times. They’re early by a tenth to four tenths of a second. ‘About’ is 0.4 seconds off. So a second model, a wav2vec2 character CTC model, re-times every word with Viterbi forced alignment. Those are the white boxes, and they sit on the actual sound.”

Click: “That model has no language model: it spells literally what it hears. So when Whisper smooths away an um, the character model still spells it, here as ‘ON’. An edit-distance alignment finds words it can only explain as extras, and if they’re spelled like a filler, we put the um back.”

Detail if asked: the DP charges 0.6 to insert a filler-spelled token vs 1.0 for any other word, and 2·(1 − similarity) to substitute; split-word pieces (“AM”+“AZON”) are glued back first. The figure is this exact clip: Whisper and alignment times from the engine’s code, run on CPU.

Architecture
Step 2: decide what to cut

Ctrl+Voice
Is itMonday?No, wait,Wednesday?

Meaning-changing edits on 10 trick sentences: 2 → 0 → 0.

Live undo: the LLM picks where the last thought began from ≤ 5 candidates (sentence ends, pauses ≥ 0.45 s, “and / but / so…”): 351 ms.

“Step two: decide what to cut. Linguists describe a self-correction in three parts: the reparandum, the words you take back; an interregnum like ‘no, wait’; and the repair. This is Shriberg’s model, from her 1994 thesis.”

Click: “We don’t let the LLM output indices: it copies the transcript and tags what to cut, which is far more reliable. Code maps the tags back to words. Then deterministic validators: deletions only; a negation, number, date or time can only go if a repair of the same type replaces it; and each span is reshaped into Shriberg form. Anything uncertain becomes a suggestion, not a cut.”

Click: “That took us from 9 to 24 exact clean-ups out of 28, with zero meaning-changing edits on the trick sentences. And live undo, choosing where your last thought began, takes 351 milliseconds.”

Architecture
Step 3: re-say it in your voice

Ctrl+Voice

Two checks, because each one can be fooled:

1. Whisper re-transcribes the spliced window and must hear the new words.

2. Whisper guesses from context. The CTC model can’t, and it must prefer the new words:

log P(new | audio) − log P(old | audio) > 0

For “specific” the margin was +29.4.

“Step three: re-say a word in your voice, only the word. If you meant ‘specific’ and said ‘pacific’, we re-say it with the word before it, ‘more specific’, because alone the new word kept the old onset.”

“We cut a window of up to four seconds at word gaps, and set the target length from your own speaking rate. Two voice-editing models, PlayDiffusion and F5-TTS, fill in the gap in parallel. We force-align their output, keep only the new words, cut at the quietest 10 milliseconds, match the loudness, and splice with an equal-power crossfade.”

Click: “Then we verify, twice. Whisper must hear the new words. But Whisper guesses from context, so the character model, which can’t, must score the new words above the old ones. If both pass we keep the candidate whose voice is closest to yours, measured with an ECAPA speaker embedding. If not, new seed, up to three rounds. Nothing unverified is sent.”

Detail: if no candidate passes after 3 rounds, the engine returns the best one with verified: false; the app only auto-applies verified replacements.

Architecture
Step 4: catch mispronunciations

Ctrl+Voice

Correlation with expert scores: 0.53, level with published GOPT (0.53); Azure 0.62 and 3MH 0.68 are higher.

In free speech the recognizer often writes what you said (“boomings”), so a text check catches those instead.

“Step four: catch the words that came out wrong. We know which sounds you should have made: espeak turns the word into phones. A wav2vec2 phoneme model listens, and CTC alignment lines up each phone. Then a ‘goodness of pronunciation’ score: how much better does the expected phone explain the audio than the best wrong phone or a deletion. That’s one batched CTC loss.”

“A gradient-boosting model on top, trained on speechocean762 expert scores, predicts the 0-to-10 score an expert would give each word.”

Click: “AUC 0.88 against expert labels, versus 0.73 without training. And if you get the same sound wrong every time, that’s your accent: we turn it into a tip, not an error.”

Honest, if asked: that’s measured on read sentences where the intended words are known. In free speech the recognizer often writes what you actually said, e.g. “boomings”, and then the text checks (misspoken/grammar) catch it; the audio detector alone flagged 19% of expert-labelled errors in our free-run test.

Architecture
Step 5: tone and accent

Ctrl+Voice

Confident edits your own recording with Praat PSOLA. No neural regeneration.

f′ = median · (f / median)range · shift

A phrase ending that rises more than 12% (uptalk) is bent into a gentle fall. Pauses over 0.45 s are squeezed; speech that trails off is sped up.

−1.5 semitones, 23% shorter, every word kept, voice similarity 0.95.

“Step five: tone. Say you’re tired. ‘Confident’ doesn’t regenerate anything: it edits your own recording with Praat’s PSOLA. Here’s the pitch of the tired take.” (Tired plays; grey curve.) “Hear the ending go up on ‘Monday?’”

Click: “Confident.” (Plays; cyan curve.) “Lower, quicker, and the rising ending is bent into a fall, so it sounds like a statement. Same words, same voice.”

Click: “And optionally, a native accent: Whisper gets the words, F5-TTS says them with a native reference voice, and Seed-VC converts that back to your timbre. It’s off by default, and the app labels it as AI.”
